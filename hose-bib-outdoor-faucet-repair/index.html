A leaking or frozen-then-cracked outdoor faucet is a quick, affordable repair. We fix and replace hose bibs and outdoor spigots for Brea homes. If you have more than one outdoor faucet showing wear, it's often worth addressing them together in one visit rather than waiting for each to fail separately.

Why This Happens In Brea.

Local Cause.

Brea's mild climate means outdoor faucets get heavy year-round use for irrigation, pools, and outdoor living rather than sitting dormant part of the year. That means wear on the internal washer and valve tends to show up as a slow drip before a full failure, worth fixing early.

Olinda Village's larger, more landscaped lots typically have more outdoor faucets in use for irrigation and horse property maintenance, which means more wear points to check during a single service call.

Signs To Watch For.

Symptoms.

  • Dripping from the spigot when off.
  • leaking from the wall behind the faucet when in use.
  • handle is hard to turn or won't shut off fully.
  • reduced flow at the outdoor faucet.
  • visible corrosion on the fitting.

How we diagnose it: We check the washer and valve seat first, since that's the most common failure point, and inspect the wall connection for a leak that shows up specifically when the faucet is in use. We also check for backflow risk at the connection, since an outdoor faucet without proper protection can be a path for contamination into the home's water supply.

FAQs.

Why does my outdoor faucet leak only when it's turned on?
That typically points to a leak at the wall connection rather than the spigot valve itself, which we can usually repair without opening the wall from the inside.
Should I be worried about a slow drip from an outdoor faucet?
It's a small fix now, but a slow drip against a wall or foundation over time can contribute to moisture damage, so it's worth addressing before it becomes a bigger problem.
What's a frost-proof hose bib and do I need one in Brea?
A frost-proof hose bib has a longer stem that shuts the water off further inside the wall, reducing freeze risk. Brea's mild climate makes hard freezes uncommon, but a frost-proof unit is still a reasonable upgrade if you're replacing one anyway.
