A pool that's losing water faster than normal evaporation usually has a leak in the plumbing lines, the equipment pad, or a fitting, not the pool shell itself in most cases. We locate and repair pool plumbing leaks for Brea's residential pools.

Why This Happens In Brea.

Local Cause.

Brea's larger-lot neighborhoods like Olinda Village and the Brea Hills have meaningful residential pool density. Pool plumbing leaks here are most commonly found at equipment pad fittings and underground line connections rather than the pool structure itself, which is why we start diagnosis at the equipment pad before assuming a shell issue.

Olinda Village's larger lots with older pool installations are more likely to have aging equipment pad plumbing than the newer pool builds we see in some of Blackstone's more recent construction.

Signs To Watch For.

Symptoms.

  • Water level dropping faster than normal evaporation (more than about 1/4 inch per day).
  • Wet spots in the yard near pool equipment.
  • Air in the pump system.
  • Higher water bill during pool season.

How we diagnose it: We conduct a bucket test to confirm actual leak rate versus normal evaporation, then pressure-test the plumbing lines and inspect equipment pad fittings before assuming the leak is in the pool shell.

Common Question.

How Do You Know If Your Pool Actually Has a Leak?.

Pools normally lose about a quarter inch of water per day to evaporation, more in hot, dry weather. If you're losing noticeably more than that, or seeing wet spots near the equipment pad, that points to an actual leak rather than normal evaporation. A bucket test comparing pool water loss to a bucket of water on the pool steps confirms it before you pay for a full leak detection service.

What To Expect.

On The Visit.

We start with a bucket test to confirm actual leak rate against normal evaporation, then check the equipment pad and pressure-test the plumbing lines before assuming the leak is in the pool shell itself, which is rarely the actual cause. Timing.

Emergency Call or Schedule It?.

Noticeable water loss beyond normal evaporation is worth investigating within a week or two, especially heading into summer when the cost adds up faster. It's rarely a same-day emergency unless the loss is severe and sudden. Either way, call (714) 750-8637 and we'll tell you honestly which one applies.

Before We Arrive.

What To Do While You Wait.

  • Run a bucket test before assuming a leak: mark the water level, place a bucket of water on the pool steps at the same level, and compare loss after 24 hours.
  • Check the equipment pad for wet spots or pooling, a common early sign of a fitting leak.
  • Avoid excessive chemical dosing to compensate for water loss, address the leak first.

FAQs.

How much water loss is normal for a pool?
About a quarter inch per day from evaporation, more in hot or windy weather. Losing significantly more than that suggests an actual leak.
Can you fix a pool leak without draining the whole pool?
Often yes, especially for equipment pad and fitting leaks. Underground line repairs sometimes require partial draining, which we'll explain before starting.
Can a pool leak damage my house or yard?
Yes, an underground line leak near the house can undermine soil or even affect a nearby foundation over time if left unaddressed, which is part of why we recommend investigating unexplained water loss promptly.
