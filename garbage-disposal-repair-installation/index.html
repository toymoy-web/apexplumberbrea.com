A jammed, leaking, or dead garbage disposal is a quick fix in most cases. We repair existing units and install new ones sized correctly for your household's actual use. A disposal that's leaking rather than just jammed shouldn't be ignored, since water reaching the motor housing or the cabinet below can turn an inexpensive repair into a bigger cleanup. Most repairs are completed within the hour, and a straightforward replacement is usually done same-day.

Why This Happens In Brea.

Local Cause.

Original disposal units in older Brea kitchens near the downtown core and in Country Road townhomes are frequently at or past their typical 8-12 year lifespan. A unit that age is more likely to have a worn motor bearing than a simple jam, which changes whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

In Blackstone's newer kitchens, disposals are more likely to be a higher-capacity model original to the build, so a repair call there often points to a foreign object jam rather than age-related motor wear.

Signs To Watch For.

Symptoms.

  • Disposal hums but doesn't spin.
  • leaking from the bottom of the unit.
  • unusual grinding noise.
  • disposal won't turn on at all.
  • foul odor even after running water.

How we diagnose it: We check for a jam first, since that's the most common and cheapest fix, then test the motor and check the seals for a leak before recommending replacement. We also check the dishwasher drain connection while working under the sink, since a disposal and dishwasher share plumbing and a problem with one can affect the other.

FAQs.

My disposal hums but won't spin, is it broken?
Usually not permanently. That's typically a jam or a tripped reset button on the bottom of the unit, both quick fixes.
How long does a garbage disposal last?
Typically 8-12 years with normal household use. If yours is older than that and starting to leak or grind, replacement is usually more cost-effective than repair.
What should never go down a garbage disposal?
Grease, fibrous vegetables like celery, coffee grounds in large amounts, and hard items like bones or fruit pits. These are common causes of jams and premature motor wear.
