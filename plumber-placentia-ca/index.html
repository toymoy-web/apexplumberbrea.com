A Small City With Two Water Systems.

Placentia lies just south of Brea, a short drive down Kraemer Boulevard, and it is one of the closest communities we serve outside our home city limits. The Old Town district along Bradford Avenue is the historic heart of town.

What makes Placentia unusual for plumbing is its water. Local Housing.

Old Town Homes And 1950s Tract Streets.

Placentia grew from a citrus-packing town into a suburb, and the housing reflects both stages. Around Old Town you find older homes from the early to mid-1900s. Most of the rest of the city was filled in with 1950s through 1970s tract homes, many on slab foundations with original galvanized or early copper supply pipe, which is where hidden slab leaks tend to show up.

What this means for pipe age: older homes near Old Town may have original cast-iron drains and galvanized supply lines, while 1950s to 1970s tract homes on slab foundations are common candidates for hidden slab leaks.

Before We Arrive.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Placentia.

Slab foundations mean a leak can run for a while before you see it. A few steps while you wait on us:

📡

Shut off the water.

Close the main valve at the front of the house or at the meter box. If you hear water running with every fixture off, that is the first test for a hidden leak.

⚡

Check the meter.

With all fixtures off, look at the meter's small leak indicator. If it keeps moving after the main is closed at the house, the leak is in the line from the street.

🛡

Protect what is on the floor.

Lift boxes, rugs, and electronics off wet areas, and watch for warm spots on the floor, which can point to a hot water line under the slab.
