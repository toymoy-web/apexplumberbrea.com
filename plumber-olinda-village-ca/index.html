The Story Here.

A Hillside Neighborhood Built On Its Own Timeline.

Olinda Village sits along Carbon Canyon Road in the hills east of the rest of Brea, on six streets built starting in 1964. It's a small neighborhood, around 120 homes, with large lots, several horse properties, and a condominium complex mixed in.

Because the whole neighborhood went up in roughly the same building window, plumbing here tends to be original 1960s-era supply lines unless a previous owner already repiped, which makes leak detection and repiping two of the calls we take most often out here. We stock the fittings for both original galvanized runs and prior repiping work, so whichever generation of plumbing your specific home has, we're usually equipped for it on the first visit.

Local Housing.

1960s Construction, Original Plumbing.

Most Olinda Village homes date to the 1960s ranch-style building wave, with a mix of original fixtures and partial updates depending on when each home last changed hands. Large lots also mean longer irrigation and outdoor line runs than a typical Brea flatland property.

What this means for pipe age: 1960s-era homes here commonly still run original galvanized or early-copper supply lines unless previously repiped.

Before We Arrive.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Olinda Village.

Original 1960s plumbing plus longer lot runs means a leak out here can travel further before it's obvious. A few steps while you're waiting on us:

📡

Shut off the water.

Larger Olinda Village lots often mean a longer walk to the main shutoff, know where yours is before you need it, usually near the meter at the street.

⚡

Kill power near the leak.

If water is anywhere near an outlet, panel, or the well pump equipment some properties out here have, cut power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach.

🛡

Clear the immediate area.

Move anything valuable away from the water, and keep horses or other animals clear of the affected area too.
