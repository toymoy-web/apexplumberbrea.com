What to Do Before an Emergency Plumber Arrives.

A few minutes of the right actions while you're waiting can meaningfully limit damage from a plumbing emergency.

By the Brea Apex Plumbing team · 2026-09-22

Need A Plumber Now?.
Call.(714) 750-8637.

Whether it's a burst pipe, a sewage backup, or a failed water heater flooding a closet, what you do in the minutes before help arrives can be the difference between a quick cleanup and a much bigger repair bill. Here's what actually helps, and what to avoid. None of this requires special tools or plumbing knowledge, just knowing what actually helps versus what just feels productive.

Shut Off the Water First.

For any active leak or burst pipe, shutting off the water is the single most effective thing you can do. Use the fixture's own shutoff valve for a localized issue, or the home's main shutoff for anything bigger or unclear in origin. See our guide on locating and using your main shutoff valve for the specifics.

Protect What You Can.

Move furniture, electronics, rugs, and boxes away from standing water or the path water is likely to spread. If it's safe to do so, place towels or a container under a smaller leak to limit spread while you wait. Don't walk through standing water near any electrical outlet or panel.

Document Before You Clean.

If you're likely to file an insurance claim, take photos or video of the damage before you start moving things or cleaning up. Insurance adjusters work from documentation, and it's much harder to reconstruct after the fact what the damage looked like in the first hour.

What Not to Do.

Don't attempt to dry a wet area with just a fan or two and call it handled, hidden moisture in walls or subfloor needs proper extraction and monitoring, not just surface drying. Don't ignore a gas smell thinking it will dissipate, that's a leave-the-house-now situation, not a wait-and-see one. And don't assume a small leak will hold until tomorrow, plumbing problems rarely improve on their own.

If Children or Pets Are in the House.

In the middle of a plumbing emergency, it's easy to focus entirely on the water and forget the basics: keep kids and pets away from any standing water, especially near outlets or the area around the water heater or furnace. If the emergency involves a gas smell, get everyone including pets out of the house immediately, gas leaks are not a situation to manage while multitasking a cleanup.

If You're Not Sure It's Actually an Emergency.

Some situations are genuinely ambiguous, a small drip versus active flooding call for different urgency levels. When in doubt, call anyway and describe what you're seeing, we can help you judge over the phone whether it warrants immediate dispatch or can reasonably wait for a scheduled visit, rather than you having to guess alone.

After the Immediate Crisis Is Handled.

Once water is shut off and the area is as protected as it can be, the waiting period is also a reasonable time to gather information that will help the repair go faster. Note when you first noticed the problem, whether anything unusual happened beforehand (renovation work, freezing weather, a recent repair elsewhere in the house), and which fixtures or areas are affected. That context, relayed when we arrive, often shortens the diagnostic time meaningfully compared to starting from zero.

Keeping a Simple Emergency Kit.

A few items kept in an accessible spot make emergency response easier: a flashlight, a pair of work gloves, towels or old linens for containing small leaks, and this guide's checklist printed or saved somewhere accessible even if your phone isn't handy. None of this replaces calling for help, but it makes the waiting period more productive and less stressful.

Quick Reference.

The First 5 Minutes.

  • Shut off water at the fixture or the main, whichever applies.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call from outside.
  • Move valuables and electronics away from water.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup starts.
  • Call for emergency service rather than waiting to see if it gets worse.
  • Keep your account or property information handy so a phone consultation can move quickly if needed.
  • Keep this guide saved somewhere accessible, like a phone note, so it's there when you actually need it.

FAQs.

Should I try to fix the problem myself before calling?
For anything beyond turning off a shutoff valve, it's generally better to stop the water and wait for a licensed plumber. DIY attempts on an active emergency sometimes make the eventual repair more complicated.
How fast can Brea Apex Plumbing respond to an emergency call?
We prioritize active emergencies for immediate dispatch and give you a realistic arrival window based on current call volume when you call.
Should I turn off my water heater during a plumbing emergency?
If the emergency doesn't involve the water heater directly, this generally isn't necessary. If it's affected or you're shutting off the whole house's water, turning off a gas water heater's control valve is a reasonable added step.
Is it safe to stay in the house during a plumbing emergency?
For most water-related emergencies, yes, as long as you're not walking through water near electrical sources. For any suspected gas leak, leave the house immediately and don't return until it's been assessed as safe.

The goal in those first minutes isn't to fix the problem, it's to stop it from getting worse until someone who can fix it arrives. Keeping this general framework in mind ahead of time means you won't be figuring out the right response for the first time under pressure.
