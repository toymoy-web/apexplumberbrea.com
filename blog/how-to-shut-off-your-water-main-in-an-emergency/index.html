How to Shut Off Your Water Main in an Emergency.

Knowing where your shutoff valve is and how to use it is the single most useful thing you can do before a plumbing emergency happens, not during one.

By the Brea Apex Plumbing team · 2026-09-22

When a pipe bursts or a fixture fails catastrophically, the first 60 seconds matter more than almost anything else. Shutting off the water fast limits damage to flooring, drywall, and belongings, and it buys time until a plumber arrives. Most Brea homeowners have never actually located their main shutoff valve until they need it in a hurry, which is exactly the wrong time to be searching.

Where to Find Your Main Shutoff Valve.

In most Brea homes, the main shutoff is near where the water line enters the house, often in a utility area, garage, or near the water meter at the curb or property line. Older homes near Downtown Brea sometimes have the valve in a less obvious spot, like a crawlspace access or an exterior wall box, so it's worth locating yours now rather than during an emergency. If you can't find an interior shutoff, the meter itself has a curb-side shutoff that any plumber, or in some cases the water utility, can access.

How to Turn It Off Correctly.

Most residential shutoff valves are either a gate valve (a round wheel-style handle, turned clockwise until it stops) or a ball valve (a lever-style handle, turned a quarter turn until it's perpendicular to the pipe). Gate valves can be stiff if they haven't been used in years, apply steady pressure rather than forcing it, since a valve that's seized can crack if it's forced hard. If a valve doesn't turn easily and you're mid-emergency, that's a case where an outdoor curb shutoff or calling us for an emergency response is the faster path.

Test It Once a Year, Not Just When You Need It.

A shutoff valve that's never been touched can seize up over years of mineral buildup around the stem. Turning it fully off and back on once a year keeps it operational and tells you now, not during a crisis, whether yours needs attention. If yours is stiff, corroded, or won't fully shut off water flow when tested, that's worth having replaced before it's the only thing standing between you and a flooded home.

What to Do After the Water's Off.

Once the main is shut, the immediate danger is contained, but the job isn't done. Open a faucet to relieve pressure in the lines, move anything valuable away from standing water, and document the damage with photos if you'll be filing an insurance claim. Then call for repair, a shutoff valve stops the bleeding, it doesn't fix the actual break.

Different Shutoff Types You Might Encounter.

Beyond the standard gate or ball valve, some Brea homes, particularly older ones near the historic downtown core, have a shutoff that requires a special key or tool to operate at the meter itself, rather than an accessible interior handle. If you've checked your property and can't find an interior valve you can operate by hand, that's worth flagging to us during a routine visit so we can identify exactly what your home has and whether an accessible interior shutoff is worth adding.

Teach Everyone in the Household, Not Just Yourself.

The person who knows where the shutoff is might not be home when an emergency happens. Walk through the location and operation with anyone else living in the house, including older kids, roommates, or anyone who stays there regularly. A shutoff valve only helps if the person present during the emergency actually knows where it is and how to use it, not just the person who read this guide.

What If the Valve Doesn't Fully Stop the Flow.

Occasionally a shutoff valve is old enough or worn enough that it slows the flow significantly but doesn't stop it completely, this is actually a sign the valve itself needs replacement, separate from whatever emergency prompted you to use it. If you find yourself in this situation, the curb-side meter shutoff is your backup, and it's worth calling us afterward to replace the faulty interior valve so it works properly next time. A valve you can't fully trust is almost as bad as not knowing where it is, since you may not realize it's compromised until you actually need it.

Quick Reference.

Before You Need It: A 2-Minute Check.

  • Locate your main shutoff valve and note exactly where it is.
  • Turn it fully closed, then fully open, to confirm it isn't seized.
  • Note whether it's a gate valve (wheel) or ball valve (lever) so you know which way to turn under pressure.
  • If it doesn't turn easily, have it inspected before it's an emergency.
  • Share the valve location with everyone in the household, not just one person.

FAQs.

What if I can't find my main shutoff valve?
Check near the water meter at the street or property line first, that's the one location every home has. If you genuinely can't locate an interior shutoff, we can help identify it during any service call.
Is it normal for a shutoff valve to be hard to turn?
Some resistance is normal on a valve that hasn't been used recently, but it shouldn't require excessive force. A valve that won't turn at all or leaks around the stem when turned is worth having replaced.
Can I install my own accessible shutoff valve if mine is hard to reach?
Yes, adding a more accessible interior shutoff is a straightforward upgrade we can do, worth considering if your only current option is a meter-side valve that requires a tool to operate.

A shutoff valve you've never tested is a gamble you don't want to lose during an actual emergency.
