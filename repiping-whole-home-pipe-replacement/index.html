When a home's internal supply lines are failing in multiple places, repiping the whole system is often more cost-effective than repeated individual repairs. We replace galvanized and polybutylene piping with modern copper or PEX. We'll also identify any fixtures or appliances that need to be temporarily disconnected during the project and walk you through the day-by-day plan before work starts.

Why This Happens In Brea.

Local Cause.

Brea homes built in the 1960s and 70s near Downtown and Country Road were built with galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside over 40-60 years. A home at or past that age with recurring leaks or declining water pressure sitewide is a strong repipe candidate rather than a series of one-off repairs.

Blackstone's 2000s-era homes were built with copper or early PEX rather than galvanized, so a full repipe there is rarely age-driven the way it is in Downtown Brea, it's more often tied to a specific defect or damage event.

Signs To Watch For.

Symptoms.

  • Recurring leaks at different points in the home.
  • Declining water pressure sitewide.
  • Rust-colored water from multiple fixtures.
  • Visible corrosion on exposed pipe sections.

How we diagnose it: We map the existing supply line layout, assess wall and ceiling access, and typically run new lines through existing wall cavities where possible to minimize drywall repair.

Options.

Repair Approaches.

🔧

PEX repiping.

Faster installation, more flexible routing, and typically lower cost than copper.

🔧

Copper repiping.

Higher upfront cost, longer proven lifespan, preferred by some homeowners for resale or personal preference.

Common Question.

How Do You Know If You Need a Full Repipe Instead of Another Repair?.

If you've had more than one or two supply-line leaks in the past couple of years, or your whole house has lower water pressure than it used to, that points to widespread internal corrosion rather than an isolated failure. We'll assess the visible pipe condition and repair history honestly rather than recommending a repipe by default.

What To Expect.

On The Visit.

We start by mapping your home's existing supply line layout and assessing wall and ceiling access, then walk you through PEX versus copper with real cost and lifespan differences for your specific home. Where possible we route new lines through existing wall cavities to minimize drywall repair, and we coordinate patching as part of the project.

Timing.

Emergency Call or Schedule It?.

This is a planned project rather than an emergency response, typically scheduled after a pattern of repeated leaks has made the case for a full repipe over continued spot repairs. Either way, call (714) 750-8637 and we'll tell you honestly which one applies.

Answered First.

FAQs.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?
Typically 2-4 days for an average single-family home, depending on size and how much drywall access is needed.
Will I have water during a repipe?
We work in stages to keep at least partial water service available where possible, and always restore full service by the end of each work day.
Will repiping damage my walls?
Some drywall access is usually necessary, but we route through existing wall cavities and minimize new openings where possible. We coordinate drywall patching as part of the project or work with your contractor.

A repipe is a significant investment, so we'll only recommend it when the repair history and pipe condition genuinely support it over continued spot repairs. If the problem is specifically the underground line from the meter to the house rather than internal plumbing, see water line repair and replacement instead. This is a common need in the older parts of Downtown Brea with original galvanized supply lines.

Parts and labor on completed repiping & whole-home pipe replacement work are covered under our standard workmanship warranty, and every job gets a firm, written price before we start, not an estimate that changes once the work is underway.
